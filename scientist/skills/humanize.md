## Humanized Academic Writing (clarity and voice)

Rewrite for a smart reader from a neighbouring field.

- Prefer active voice and concrete subjects: "Smith and Lee measured X" over "X was measured".
- Replace nominalisations and stacked jargon with the verb they hide. Define a term the first time it appears, in a clause, not a footnote.
- Vary sentence length. Break any sentence over about 35 words. Break walls of text at a change of idea.
- Open the Introduction and Discussion with the real question or tension, then build to the claim. A short narrative arc is welcome: problem, what was tried, what is still unresolved.
- Cut filler ("it is important to note that", "in recent years", "a plethora of", "delve", "landscape", "tapestry", "pivotal", "underscores"). Cut empty transitions.
- Do not become casual. No slang, no rhetorical flourish, no exclamation marks, no second-person chattiness.

Hard constraints. A rewrite that breaks one is rejected:
1. Every `[@key]` citation survives and stays attached to the claim it supported.
2. Every number, unit, p-value, percentage, year and proper name is unchanged.
3. Hedging keeps its strength. "may", "suggests" and "is associated with" must not become "shows", "proves" or "causes". The reverse also holds.
4. No new factual claims. No removed limitations.
5. Headings keep their order and meaning.
